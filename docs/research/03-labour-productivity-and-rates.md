# NexaFix: labour productivity norms and UAE labour cost rates (defaults for the costing engine)

Researched 2026-09-28. All figures are **editable defaults**, not guarantees.
Tags: `(domain)` = source (full URLs in section 15). `est.` = my estimate, triangulated from the cited sources and adjusted to Dubai apartment/villa fit-out.
FX: 1 USD = 3.6725 AED (peg). 1 GBP ≈ 4.9 AED (est.). 1 INR ≈ 0.043 AED (est.). 1 m² = 10.764 ft². 1 m = 3.281 ft.

---

## 0. Conventions used in every table

| Item | Default | Source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Crew-day | 8 paid hours on site (the legal normal day) | u.ae: 8 h/day, 48 h/week |
| Man-hours (mh) | Total person-hours for the whole crew per unit. RSMeans convention: mh/unit does not change with crew size; only daily output does | rsmeans.com |
| What is included | Setting out, material handling on the floor, the work itself, and daily clean-up | est. |
| What is excluded | Travel to site, lift/permit waiting beyond normal, and materials. Add these separately (section 12) | est. |
| Low / Typ / High | **Low mh** = fast (open rooms, repeat work, good substrate). **High mh** = slow (small rooms, many cuts, bad walls, occupied home). Output columns are shown as fast/typ/slow | est. |
| Quantities | Areas are net of openings over 1 m². Linear items (lm) are measured on the visible face | est. |

### Caveats
- Several UAE productivity charts on scribd, pdfcoffee and planningengineer.net were blocked; their figures come from search-result snippets only.
- US/UK sources describe more automated shops and higher-paid trades. For Dubai, man-hours were adjusted upward (×1.3–2 in workshops) and the adjustments are marked est.
- No reliable published Dubai rates were found for PU spray subcontracting, upholsterer pay, or fit-out daywork, so those rows are estimates. Get 2–3 local quotes before locking them.
- Salary aggregators vary widely (e.g. Indeed carpenter AED 1,811 vs a finishing-carpenter figure of AED 4,800). Defaults use the mid-market for firms that provide camp accommodation.
